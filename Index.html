The Risk Register · example content
The premise the design rests on

Consistency is usually not the missing ingredient. People who abandon a study plan are often demonstrably capable of sustained, difficult effort elsewhere — it simply flows to whatever reliably pays for it.

So the design goal is a scoreboard that pays for effort tonight, including a reduced “floor” version that still pays on a bad day. Long-horizon goals lose to short-horizon feedback unless you build the short-horizon feedback yourself. Every counter in this app is cumulative and none of them reset on a missed day, which is the whole point.

What the register is for: naming a failure mode in advance, with the observable tell and the counter-move, so that recognising it in the moment does not depend on insight you will not have at the time.

The 5 Non-Negotiable Rules
R1

The Alarm Is Not a Question

▼

Rule: Wake 07:15. Feet on the floor inside the 07:15–07:20 alarm window. No debate. No evaluation. No phone.

Bright line: Still horizontal at 07:21 = violated. No grey area.

Corrective: Log "OVERSLEPT" in the Firewall. Execute the full Boot Sequence anyway — late, imperfect, executed. If it repeats, the cause is upstream: check the 22:30 shutdown and the bedtime.

R2

Work Surface Before Escape Surface

▼

Rule: The day starts on the work surface, not the escape surface. The certification laptop opens to the pre-laid-out task before the phone is unlocked. During 09:00–10:30 and the 13:00–14:00 lunch block the phone is in another room.

Bright line: Unlocking the screen (not just silencing the alarm) before the work surface is open = violated.

Corrective: Phone into a drawer for the rest of the block. Log "PHONE BREACH". Three violations in a week → the phone charges in a different room, permanently.

This is the single most important rule. Follow only this and the Builder, the Architect of Defaults, the One Who Stays and the Metronome all engage almost on their own.

R3

Nothing Ends Without Something Saved

▼

Rule: No focus block ends without something saved to show for it — a worked step, code re-explained in my own words, notes, a lab output, a practice answer. And every commitment row is marked D, M or X before sleep. No blank rows.

Bright line: A session logged with an empty "what's saved" = the session went through the motions. Going to sleep with any unmarked row = violated. Even all-X beats blank.

Corrective: Get up, walk to the desk, save the thing, mark the rows, write one sentence. The inconvenience is the point.

R4

The 14-Day Lock

▼

Rule: The Daily Deep-Work Session is locked for 14 days from its first run (1 September 2026). Content — which task, which track — can change. Architecture cannot.

Bright line: Opening a blank page to draft a "better version" inside the lock = the redesign loop. The real risk is not the design; it is day 3 at 08:37 thinking "this sheet could be a little better." That moment is what the sheet exists for.

Corrective: Close the page. Log "REDESIGN URGE". Execute what exists. Changes happen at a review point, on evidence, as a parameter tweak — never mid-week, on mood, as a rebuild.

Test: adjusting timing, which block, or the floor threshold = adapting. Regenerating because it feels off = the loop.

R5

Never Miss Twice

▼

Rule: After a miss, execute at least the bad-day floor the very next day — and do not open the plan to redesign. Two consecutive zero days does not happen.

Bright line: Two consecutive days with zero D or M = violated. One bad day + one floor day = within the rules.

Corrective: Layer 5. Fire the smallest version the next day and mark it green. Add nothing, rebuild nothing. Three or more → restart at the floor for the whole week and bring it to the Sunday review as evidence.

Perfection was never the standard; recovery speed is. Who I am is defined by how fast I return, not by whether I ever fall.

Backup Self-Talk — the secondary lever
Use these only when the environment and systems haven't already made the action automatic. The primary levers are the surroundings and the pre-committed systems — not talking yourself into it.
When the reflex fires

“This is the reflex, not me. The discomfort is the work. I don't need to feel like it — I need to do the next small thing. Ten more minutes. I am the one who stays.”

When the pull is to redesign the plan

“The plan is fine. Executing it is the hard part — and that's the part I'm doing right now. I'll note the idea for Sunday.”

Internal Coach Scripts
S1

The Alarm Negotiation

▼

You have lost this negotiation a hundred times. Not once has "10 more minutes" turned into a productive morning. The debate is rigged — the version of you in bed always wins because he has zero interest in your goals. 5-4-3-2-1. Stand up.

S2

The "Just Today" Excuse

▼

You know exactly where "just today" leads. It has never been one day. It was never one day. "Just today" is the Promise Breaker's signature move — and you have fallen for it every single time. Do the minimum. Log the M. Move.

S3

The Post-Miss Spiral

▼

The day is not ruined. You missed one thing. That is an X in one column, not a line through the entire row. Open the app. Look at what is still unmarked. Do one of those. An imperfect day with one M is still a non-zero day.

S4

The Redesign Urge

▼

You have an archive of perfect plans that were each executed for less than a week. The system is not flawed. It is uncomfortable. Those are different things. Log the urge. Close the blank page. Execute what exists. You redesign on Day 14. Not before.

S5

The Phone Pull

▼

You are not "just checking." You have never "just checked." You know exactly what happens: one notification becomes 30 minutes of nothing. Put it down. It will still be there when the system is done. Your goals will not.
